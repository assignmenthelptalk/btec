Most BTEC assignments follow the same chain: your tutor sets or issues the assignment and marks it, a different member of staff at your centre checks a sample of that marking, and Pearson's standards verifier later samples the centre's work. This page follows Pearson's Centre Guide to BTEC internal assessment (version 4.1, August 2026) to show who looks at your work, what your tutor is allowed to tell you, and what records are kept.

Who Sets and Marks Your BTEC Assignment?

For internally assessed units, your centre sets the assignment brief, your tutor (the assessor) judges your evidence against the unit's assessment criteria, and the result is recorded and confirmed. Pearson says centres are free to design their own assignment briefs, and it also provides authorised assignment briefs. For the BTEC Nationals from 2025, Pearson writes the assignment briefs, with no adaptations allowed except to hand-out and hand-in dates, and your centre marks them.

Pearson also says an assignment should not be issued until students are ready to work independently with no further input from the assessor. Homework and mock assignments can be used to judge readiness.

What Your Tutor Can and Cannot Do

Pearson splits feedback into three stages, and the rules are different at each.

  • Before the assessment. Your tutor must make sure you understand the assessment requirements, the evidence you need to produce, and the importance of time management and deadlines. Pearson says tutors can set "dry run" or mock tasks, explain how Pass, Merit and Distinction work, and give predicted grades. Predicted grades are an indicator only, and not a formal assessment.
  • During the assessment. You work independently. Your tutor can clarify what the assignment brief requires, confirm which criteria the assessor is targeting, and give general guidance. Your tutor should not assess your evidence as you produce it, confirm that you have met specific criteria, "coach" you to produce the evidence, or give you a specific list of actions to meet the criteria or reach a grade.
  • After the assessment. You receive feedback on the result. Pearson says feedback must reach you close to the assessment date, and an assessor may not hold it back until the following term or the end of the year.

The resources you can use while working on an assignment are the knowledge and skills from your teaching, handouts and learning resources, the unit content and assessment criteria in the specification, and the assignment brief itself.

What Is Internal Verification (IV)?

Internal verification is how a centre checks that assessment decisions have been made accurately. Pearson's rules are:

  • An assessor cannot internally verify their own decisions. Another member of staff must act as the internal verifier, ideally someone with the subject knowledge or experience of assessing BTECs.
  • The centre plans internal verification at the start of the course, including when it will check assignment briefs, first submissions and resubmissions.
  • Every assignment must be internally verified after it is submitted, and not only at the end of the unit, and the sample must include student work from all assessors teaching the unit.
  • There is no requirement to verify every student. The sample must include students who were awarded each grade, and its size should reflect risk, such as how experienced the assessor is, whether the unit is new, and past standards verification outcomes.

The Lead Internal Verifier also decides whether a resubmission or retake can go ahead; see our resubmission guide.

How Pearson Checks the Centre: Standards Verification

Pearson's own term for the outside check on internally assessed units is standards verification, carried out by Pearson standards verifiers. The Centre Guide says the window for standards verification runs from January, and a centre must have its samples ready to share with its verifier by 15 May, so centres need completed units available for sampling by then. Standards verifiers can ask for particular work, including resubmissions and retakes, and the centre must then produce the original work, the assessment records, the authorisation for the resubmission or retake and the internal verification documentation.

Because Pearson samples work across the centre, it does not look at every student's submission. For this reason the centre's internal verification and your tutor's records matter: they are the evidence that your grade was awarded to the national standard.

How Your Unit Grade Is Decided and What Is Recorded

Your assessor formally records the achievement of each criterion on an assessment record, which also captures your authentication declaration, the assessor's feedback and the assessor's own authenticity declaration. Pearson's rule for the unit grade is the highest level for which you have met all the criteria. To achieve a Distinction you must satisfy all the Distinction criteria and all the Pass and Merit criteria, and to achieve a Merit you must satisfy all the Merit criteria and all the Pass criteria.

Centres must keep student work for 12 weeks after certificates are received, and keep assignment briefs, assessment records, internal verification documents and achievement tracking for at least three years. If you may need to appeal, ask your centre in writing to keep your records.

What if you disagree with your assessment result? The checks above are designed to catch inconsistency, but a formal route exists if you believe your result was affected by a failure to follow procedures.

If You Think Something Went Wrong

Raise it with your centre first, following its appeals procedure. Pearson accepts an appeal from a learner only after the centre's own procedure is complete, and not simply because you disagree with the grade. See BTEC internal verification appeal for the stages and time limits, and BTEC assignment submission rules for what Pearson requires when you hand work in.

Does Pearson mark every BTEC internally assessed assignment?

No. Pearson's standards verifiers sample work from each centre. They do not review every student's work.

Can my grade change after internal verification?

It can. Internal verification exists to check that assessment decisions are accurate, so a decision can be corrected if the internal verifier and assessor find it was wrong. Your centre should tell you if your result changes.

What's the difference between internal verification and standards verification?

Internal verification is your centre's own check, carried out by a member of staff who did not make the original decision. Standards verification is Pearson's check on a sample of the centre's work, with samples due to the verifier by 15 May. Some units are instead moderated by Pearson itself.

Can I see the result of Pearson's standards verification of my centre?

Standards verification reports are written for the centre, so students do not normally see them. If you think a result of yours was affected, ask your centre, and see our appeal page if you believe procedures were not followed.
