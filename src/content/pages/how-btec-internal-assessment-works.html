Most BTEC assignments follow the same marking chain: your tutor marks first, a second assessor at your centre checks that marking, and Pearson samples the centre's overall standard afterward. This page walks through each stage, so you know exactly who is looking at your work and when.

Who Marks Your BTEC Assignment?

Your own course tutor sets the Assignment Brief and marks your submitted evidence against the Pass, Merit, and Distinction criteria for that unit.

What Is Internal Verification (IV)?

A second, more experienced assessor at the same centre samples a proportion of marked work across the cohort to check that the tutor's marking is accurate and consistent, before results are confirmed to students. This step catches inconsistencies between different tutors marking the same unit, or between different cohorts at the same centre.

How Pearson's External Moderation Works

After internal confirmation, Pearson samples the centre's overall portfolio of marked work, not every individual student's submission, to verify that the centre's marking standard matches the national benchmark for that unit. If a centre's standard doesn't match, Pearson can require remarking before results are finalised.

Standardisation: How Tutors Agree on Marking Before They Start

Centres hold standardisation meetings where tutors marking the same unit align their interpretation of the criteria using sample work, reducing marker-to-marker inconsistency before assessment even begins. This is why two tutors at the same centre should reach very similar conclusions when marking equivalent evidence.

What if you disagree with your assessment result? The marking chain above is designed to catch inconsistency, but it isn't infallible, and there is a formal route if you believe your specific result was affected by a genuine error.

What Happens If Pearson's Moderation Finds a Discrepancy?

If a centre's marking standard doesn't match the national benchmark, Pearson can require remarking or adjust grades centre-wide before results are finalised. This is a centre-level quality process, separate from an individual student appeal. See BTEC internal verification appeal if you believe your own specific result was marked incorrectly.

Does Pearson mark every BTEC internally assessed assignment?

No. Pearson samples a proportion of each centre's marked portfolio for moderation. It does not remark every individual student's work.

Can my grade change after internal verification?

Yes. If the internal verifier identifies a marking inconsistency, your tutor's original mark can be adjusted before your grade is confirmed to you.

What's the difference between internal verification and moderation?

Internal verification happens within your own centre before results are confirmed. External moderation is Pearson's separate check on the centre's overall marking standard afterward.

How do I know if my centre passed Pearson's moderation?

This is typically a centre-level administrative outcome not routinely shared with individual students, though a failed moderation that affects your grade would normally be communicated by your centre.
