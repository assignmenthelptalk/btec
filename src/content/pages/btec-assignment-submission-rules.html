Pearson sets a small number of firm rules about how BTEC assignments are submitted, and leaves most of the practical detail, such as word counts, file formats and the exact deadline, to your centre and your assignment brief. This page separates the two, using Pearson's Centre Guide to BTEC internal assessment (version 4.1, August 2026), so you know which rules are fixed and which questions to put to your assessor.

What Pearson Requires You to Submit

For each assignment, the Centre Guide says that:

  • Only one submission is allowed for each assignment. A resubmission is a separate, authorised opportunity, not a second go at will.
  • You submit evidence towards the assessment criteria the assignment targets.
  • You sign and date a declaration of authenticity with each assignment, confirming you produced the evidence yourself.
  • Work is submitted in English unless prior approval has been obtained.

Electronic signatures are permitted on assessment documents if the centre has an audit trail, and a typed or scanned signature can be used on online documents. Your tutor will tell you how your centre handles this.

Deadlines and Extensions

Pearson describes setting and meeting deadlines as an essential part of BTEC delivery, and says students should not be advantaged by having extra time. In practice that means:

  • Your centre should have a clear procedure for applying for an extension if you have genuine reasons for missing a deadline, such as illness. It should be explained at induction.
  • If an extension is granted, the new deadline applies and should be recorded.
  • If you cannot use an extension, it may be appropriate to ask for special consideration under Pearson's reasonable adjustments and special consideration guidance.

Ask for an extension before the deadline, in the way your centre requires, and keep a copy of the request.

Is late work penalised?

The Centre Guide says that once evidence is accepted for assessment, students are not to be penalised purely for submitting late, unless that is explicitly part of the Merit or Distinction criteria or the assessment guidance. It also says that only the unit's assessment criteria can be used for assessment decisions. So a late mark deduction of the kind used at some universities is not what the guide describes.

Do not read this as permission to submit late. Missing the deadline has a real consequence in another place: a resubmission can be authorised only if you met the original deadline or an agreed extension, and the guide says a student who misses the initial submission date has no opportunity for resubmission or retake.

The Declaration of Authenticity and AI

The declaration is how you confirm that the work is your own. Assessors should only accept authentic evidence, and the centre is responsible for validating it. If an assessor doubts that work is yours, they must not sign their own authenticity declaration, and the centre follows its malpractice policy and reports suspected malpractice to Pearson where required.

On artificial intelligence, the guide repeats the JCQ position:

  • All work submitted for assessment must be your own.
  • If you misuse AI so that submitted work is not your own, that is malpractice and may attract severe sanctions.
  • If any section is reproduced directly from AI-generated responses, you must identify it, and you should understand that it cannot show you have met the criteria independently, so it will not be rewarded.
  • If a teacher suspects unacknowledged AI use, they must investigate and take appropriate action.

Pearson's authenticity declarations were updated to include a notice about acknowledging AI-generated content. Read the wording on the form your centre gives you before signing. Word Count, Format and Length

Pearson's Centre Guide does not set a word count, page limit or file format for BTEC assignments. We have not found a general Pearson rule, so any figure you see quoted as "the BTEC word count" should be checked against your own brief.

These details are normally set in the assignment brief or by your centre. Look for:

  • A stated length or time for a written, presentation or practical task.
  • The form of evidence the brief asks for, for example a report, a poster, a witness statement or a recording.
  • Required file types, naming conventions and the platform you upload to.
  • Whether tables, appendices and references count toward any limit.

If the brief does not say, ask your assessor in writing before you submit. Meeting the criteria is what decides the grade, so cover each task with enough depth to show the criterion is met, and do not pad to reach a number. A Pre-Submission Checklist

  • Every task in the brief has a section in your work, and the command verb in each task is answered.
  • The work is your own, sources are referenced, and any AI use is acknowledged as your centre requires.
  • The declaration is signed and dated.
  • The file follows the type, name and length the brief or centre asks for.
  • You are submitting on or before the deadline, or you hold a recorded extension.
  • You have kept a copy and a record of when and how you submitted.

Not sure what your brief expects? A request to your assessor for clarification, made before the deadline and in writing, costs nothing and protects you if there is a disagreement later.

If Something Goes Wrong After Submission

If you disagree with a grade, the centre's own appeals procedure comes first. Our page on the BTEC internal verification appeal explains the stages. If you did not pass first time, read the BTEC resubmission guide.

Is there a word count for BTEC assignments?

Pearson's Centre Guide does not set one. Length is normally set by the assignment brief or your centre, so check your brief and ask your assessor if it is not stated.

Can I submit a BTEC assignment late?

Ask for an extension before the deadline. Pearson says that accepted work is not penalised purely for being late unless the criteria say so, but a student who misses the original deadline without an approved extension has no opportunity for resubmission or retake.

Can I use AI in a BTEC assignment?

The work you submit must be your own. Pearson says that passages reproduced directly from AI must be identified and will not be rewarded, and that misuse which makes the work not your own is malpractice. Follow your centre's rules on acknowledging AI.
