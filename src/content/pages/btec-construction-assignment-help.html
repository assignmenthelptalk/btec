BTEC Construction Assignment Formats: Reports, Surveys, and Design Specifications

Common evidence types include technical reports, site survey analysis with written commentary, design specifications, and health and safety risk assessments. Each Assignment Brief specifies the exact format expected, and following it precisely matters as much as the technical content itself.

What Distinction Requires in BTEC Construction Assignments

Distinction-level work evaluates trade-offs, such as material cost against durability against sustainability, rather than describing a single design choice in isolation. Justified recommendations that weigh several options against UK Building Regulations and relevant health and safety legislation are what separate Distinction from Pass in this subject.

BTEC Construction Units and Assessment: What Pearson's Specifications Say

Pearson currently publishes two generations of the BTEC Level 3 National in Construction and the Built Environment: the Alternative Academic Qualification (AAQ) Extended Certificate, first taught from September 2025, and the 2017 specification suite, which includes the Diploma and Extended Diploma. Unit titles and assessment differ between them, so check with your tutor which one your centre runs before you read a brief against this page.

AAQ Extended Certificate (first teaching September 2025)

  • It is 360 guided learning hours (GLH). Students complete five units from six: three mandatory and two optional.
  • The mandatory units are Unit 1 Construction Principles, Unit 2 Construction Technology and Unit 3 Design for Construction and the Built Environment. Units 1 and 2 are written examinations set and marked by Pearson, and Unit 3 is internally assessed.
  • The Unit 1 exam is 90 marks and lasts 1 hour 45 minutes. The Unit 2 exam is 70 marks and also lasts 1 hour 45 minutes. Both are available in January and May/June, and external assessment makes up 50% of the qualification GLH.
  • The optional units are all internally assessed: Unit 4 Construction Commercial Management, Unit 5 Retrofit in Construction and the Built Environment and Unit 6 Modelling in Construction. Students complete two.
  • The Unit 2 and Unit 3 assessments also test underpinning knowledge from Unit 1, so Unit 1 content carries across the qualification.

2017 suite (for example the Extended Diploma)

  • The Extended Diploma has nine mandatory units, six internal and three external. External assessment makes up 33% of the total GLH.
  • The external units are Unit 1 Construction Principles (a written exam of 1 hour 30 minutes and 75 marks), Unit 2 Construction Design and Unit 3 Tendering and Estimating. Units 2 and 3 are tasks set and marked by Pearson and completed under supervised conditions.
  • Internally assessed units in the specification include Unit 4 Construction Technology, Unit 5 Health and Safety in Construction, Unit 6 Surveying in Construction, Unit 7 Graphical Detailing in Construction, Unit 8 Building Regulations and Control in Construction and Unit 9 Management of a Construction Project.
  • Smaller qualifications in the same suite have different mandatory units, so check the one you are studying.

Externally assessed units are taken by you under Pearson's conditions, so the guidance on this page about assignments applies to the internally assessed units. For how external assessments work, see BTEC external assessment explained.

Source: Pearson's specifications for the AAQ Extended Certificate (issue 3, October 2025) and the 2017 Extended Diploma (issue 8, August 2022). Pearson revises specifications, so check the current issue.

Reading Construction Criteria: A Worked Example From Pearson's Specification

Internal Construction units grade each learning aim at Pass, Merit and Distinction. The AAQ Unit 3, Design for Construction and the Built Environment, shows how the same subject matter is asked for at three depths. In learning aim C you review your own building design against the client or end user brief.

  • Pass (C.P4): you review how well your chosen methods of construction, material choices, final layout, design and sustainability requirements meet the client brief.
  • Merit (C.M3): you discuss the same considerations in terms of how well they satisfy the client brief.
  • Distinction (C.D3): you evaluate those considerations, justify the decisions you made and include areas for improvement.

The content is identical at all three levels. What changes is what you do with it. At Distinction, a design review that only describes what was built is not enough: you weigh your choices against the brief, explain why you made them and say what you would improve.

The same pattern appears in learning aim A. At Pass you outline the factors from the client brief that affect the design, including sustainability requirements. At Merit you assess them with supported reasons and ways to overcome limitations. At Distinction you evaluate them, informed by research, justifying how the limitations can be overcome. Learning aim B asks you to produce the design itself, using manual techniques and CAD with annotated sketches, supported by a design statement that shows how the design satisfies the brief.

What kind of evidence do BTEC Construction assignments require?

Typically technical reports, site survey analysis, design specifications, and health and safety risk assessments, depending on the specific unit.

Does BTEC Construction assignment help cover health and safety units?

Yes. Health and safety in construction is a core unit area and is covered alongside the technical and design-focused units.

Can BTEC Construction lead to a career without going further than Level 3?

Pearson says the Foundation Diploma supports progression to an Apprenticeship in the construction sector or a further year of Level 3 study, and that the Diploma may be combined with other BTEC Nationals or A levels to support progression to higher education courses in construction areas before entering employment. Pearson's specification also lists Higher Apprenticeships as a progression route in the suite. Check the specification for your size and ask your centre.
