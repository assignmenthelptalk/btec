BTEC uses criterion-referenced grading. Every unit's Assignment Brief lists explicit criteria labelled P1, P2, M1, M2, D1, D2, and a student's grade on that unit depends entirely on which of those criteria they satisfy, not on a percentage mark. This page explains what Pass, Merit, and Distinction actually mean, how a qualification-level grade is built from individual unit results, and clears up the single most common confusion in BTEC grading: what Distinction* really is.

What Pass, Merit, and Distinction Mean at Unit Level

Each unit's Assignment Brief lists a specific set of criteria for each grade band. A student's unit grade is the highest band where every criterion at that band, and every band below it, has been met. Meeting some Distinction criteria while missing a Pass criterion still results in a Referral for that unit, not a partial award.

This matters because it removes any notion of grades blending into each other. A response can be full of insight and still fail to achieve Merit if it doesn't explicitly satisfy every Pass criterion first.

Criterion-Referenced Grading vs Percentage Marking

Exam-based qualifications typically award marks on a sliding scale, where getting close to the right answer still earns partial credit. BTEC doesn't work that way. Either the specific evidence a criterion asks for is present in your submission, or it isn't, which is why checking your work against the exact criteria list matters more than general essay polish.

How Unit Grades Combine Into an Overall Qualification Grade

For multi-unit qualifications like the Diploma or Extended Diploma, each unit contributes points weighted by its size, measured in Guided Learning Hours. Those points total up to a specific grade combination, such as DDM or D*DD, rather than an average. A single weak unit can pull the overall combination down even if every other unit reaches Distinction.

What Is BTEC Distinction*? Qualification-Level vs Unit-Level Grades

There is no such thing as a D* on an individual unit. Distinction* exists only as an overall qualification grade on the Extended Diploma, awarded when a student meets a specific proportion of Distinction-grade units alongside a points threshold across the whole qualification. Getting every single unit to Distinction is one route to D*D*D*, but the calculation is based on the overall points total, not simply "all Distinctions."

Can you get a D* on a single BTEC unit?

No. Distinction* only exists as an overall qualification grade on the Extended Diploma, calculated from a mix of unit grades and total points, and is never awarded per unit.

What happens if I meet Distinction criteria but miss one Pass criterion?

You receive a Referral for that unit, not a Pass. Distinction-quality work does not compensate for an unmet Pass criterion, because all lower-band criteria must be satisfied first.

Is a BTEC Merit equivalent to a B grade at A-level?

There's no direct grade-for-grade translation. BTEC and A-level use fundamentally different grading logic, though UCAS assigns comparable tariff points to specific grade combinations from each.

How many units do I need at Distinction to get an overall Distinction*?

It depends on your qualification size and how each unit is weighted. Pearson's official grade calculator for your specific qualification gives the exact combination required.
