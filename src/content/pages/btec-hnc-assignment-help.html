This page covers the Pearson BTEC Level 4 Higher National Certificate (HNC). It sets out what the qualification is, how it is assessed, how it is graded, and what Pearson says happens if an assignment is not passed. It follows Pearson's Higher Nationals in Business specification (Issue 3, January 2022, first teaching September 2021), the Higher National suite we can check against a published specification. Other Higher National subjects have their own specifications, so check the one for your programme. For the Level 5 Diploma, see our HND page.

What the BTEC HNC Is

Pearson's Higher Nationals are Level 4 to Level 5 qualifications built for students who want a clear line of sight to employment and to a degree at Level 6 if they choose. The HNC is the Level 4 qualification. Pearson says it provides a solid grounding in business that students can build on if they continue their studies.

The HND includes 120 credits of Level 4 study alongside its Level 5 units. Pearson says each university sets its own entry criteria, so check how your centre and any progression partner treat the HNC.

The HNC Units

The Business HNC has six core units, all mandatory, and two optional units, all worth 15 credits.

Optional units at Level 4 include Recording Financial Transactions, Operations Management, Business Law and Digital Business in Practice. Which options you study depends on your centre and, at HND, your pathway. Each unit specification lists its learning outcomes and a Pass, Merit and Distinction grading grid. For example, one unit's grid pairs a Pass criterion such as "Explain..." with a Merit criterion such as "Analyse..." and a Distinction criterion such as "Critically analyse...". Your Assignment Brief tells you which criteria each task targets.

How the HNC Is Assessed

Pearson describes two kinds of assessment in the Higher Nationals: internal assignments that centres set and assess, and Pearson-set assignments that centres set in line with Pearson's guidelines. Centres grade both. The specification does not describe Pearson-set examinations.

For the HNC there is one compulsory Pearson-set unit, Unit 6 Managing a Successful Business Project. Pearson decides a theme each year, and the centre sets the project brief based on that theme. Pearson says the unit is meant to give centres a common framework so it can compare information across the sector. The unit asks you to explain the stages of the project lifecycle, produce a Project Management Plan using primary and secondary research, implement the plan and draw conclusions from the evidence, and reflect on the value gained.

Pearson's specification does not set a word count or a referencing system. Your centre tells you what its assignments require. Pearson does say centres should give students a guide that explains how to use and reference source materials, including how to avoid plagiarism, so follow your centre's guide. See also our page on referencing for BTEC HNC and HND.

HNC Grading

Each unit is graded Pass, Merit or Distinction against the criteria in its specification. The overall qualification grade is worked out from points. Pearson's table gives 4 points per credit for a Pass, 6 for a Merit and 8 for a Distinction, and the overall grade follows the point boundaries below.

To be awarded the HNC, a student must complete units worth 120 credits at Level 4 and achieve at least a Pass in 105 of those credits. Students who attempted but did not achieve a Pass in one 15-credit unit can still be awarded the HNC if they completed and passed the remaining units. That unit appears as unclassified on the notification of performance.

What separates Merit from Distinction in an HNC assignment? The answer is in the criteria your brief targets. Read each command verb, such as explain, analyse or critically analyse, and check that every part of your answer does what that verb asks for.

If an HNC Assignment Is Not Passed

Pearson says that if a student's assignment does not reach a Pass, they must have the opportunity to resubmit it, once only. The grade for a reassessed assignment is capped at a Pass, and work that already achieved a Pass or higher cannot be reassessed. If a student still fails the unit after reassessment, they may be allowed to repeat the unit once, again capped at a Pass. How your centre schedules resubmissions is in its assessment policy.

Progression from the HNC

Pearson says the Level 5 HND allows students to specialise and progress toward degree-level study, and it describes the Level 4 HNC as the grounding that students can build on if they continue. Pearson also says universities each set their own admission criteria, which can change, and that some allow advanced entry to the second or third year. Check the course page of the university you are interested in. Pearson points students to its degree finder at highernationals.com for the current picture.

Is Harvard referencing required for BTEC HNC assignments?

Pearson's Higher Nationals specification does not set a referencing system. It says centres should give students a guide that explains how to use and reference source materials, including how to avoid plagiarism. Use the style your centre or Assignment Brief names, and apply it consistently.

What is the difference between Merit and Distinction at HNC?

Each unit specification has a grading grid with Pass, Merit and Distinction criteria. In one unit, for example, a Pass asks you to explain, a Merit to analyse and a Distinction to critically analyse. The criteria differ unit by unit, so read the ones your Assignment Brief targets. The overall grade then comes from points: 4, 6 or 8 per credit for Pass, Merit or Distinction.

What happens if I fail an HNC assignment?

Pearson says you must have the opportunity to resubmit, once only, and a reassessed assignment is capped at a Pass. If you still fail the unit, you may be allowed to repeat it once, again capped at a Pass. A student who fails one 15-credit unit but passes the rest can still be awarded the HNC. Ask your centre how it schedules resubmissions.

Is the Managing a Successful Business Project unit an exam?

No. It is a Pearson-set unit, which means Pearson provides an annual theme and guidance, the centre sets the project brief, and the centre grades the work. The specification does not set a word count, so follow the length in your Assignment Brief.
