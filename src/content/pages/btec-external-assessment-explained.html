Most BTEC units are marked by your own tutor, but some are set and marked by Pearson instead. This is called external assessment. It is taken under specified conditions, it is marked by Pearson, and it comes with different rules about second attempts from internal coursework. This page uses Pearson's own specifications to explain what external assessment is, how it is run, and what the resit rules say.

What Is BTEC External Assessment?

Pearson's BTEC National specifications say that each external assessment is linked to a specific unit. It is taken under specified conditions, then marked by Pearson, and a grade is awarded. The units designed for external assessment are larger units of 60, 90 or 120 guided learning hours, so that students can show breadth and depth.

How much of a qualification is external varies. In Pearson's 2025 Construction and the Built Environment Extended Certificate, two of the three mandatory units are externally assessed, and external assessment makes up 50% of the qualification. In the older Construction and the Built Environment Diploma, Pearson's specification gives 33%. Check the assessment table in your own specification.

Exams, Set Tasks and Performance

The Travel and Tourism specification describes two styles of external assessment:

  • Examinations. All learners take the same assessment at the same time, normally with a written outcome.
  • Set tasks. Learners take the assessment during a defined window and show understanding by completing a vocational task.

Some external assessments include a period of preparation using set information. Pearson's specification also lists performance as a type of external assessment for some qualifications.

Three degrees of control

Pearson defines how tightly each external assessment is supervised:

  • High control. Formal invigilated examination conditions.
  • Medium control. Usually a longer period, which may include controlled conditions. Learners may be allowed to use resources, prepared notes or the internet to help them complete the task.
  • Low control. Activities completed without direct supervision, such as research, preparation of materials and practice. Pearson does not directly assess the materials produced under low control.

Some external assessments have more than one part, and each part can have a different degree of control. Your centre will tell you which applies to your task, and Pearson publishes Instructions for Conducting External Assessments for centres.

How to Find Out Which of Your Units Are External

Look at the assessment summary in your qualification specification, or the assessment plan your centre gives you. External units are scheduled for fixed dates, unlike coursework deadlines that your tutor sets. Pearson says its external assessments are available twice a year, and publishes the dates in its annual examinations timetable.

Which units are external changes between qualifications and between specification generations, so use your own specification and not a general list. Some examples from Pearson's documents:

  • Travel and Tourism (2019 National). Unit 1, The World of Travel and Tourism, is assessed with short- and long-answer questions about the industry. Unit 2, Global Destinations, asks learners to complete tasks on the features of global destinations and how travel plans meet customer requirements.
  • Business (2016 National). Unit 2, Developing a Marketing Campaign, was assessed through two activities worth 70 marks in total, according to Pearson's June 2018 Lead Examiner Report. Our page on examiner feedback reports summarises what that report said.
  • Construction and the Built Environment (2025 National, Extended Certificate). Two of the three mandatory units are external.

Resits: What the Specifications Say

Resits work differently from coursework resubmission, and the rules depend on the generation of the qualification.

  • 2025 Alternative Academic Qualification Nationals. Students may resit an examination twice, which makes three attempts in total including the first. If a student resits, the best grade achieved counts towards the qualification grade, not necessarily the most recent sitting.
  • Earlier Nationals, such as the 2019 Travel and Tourism specification. Learners may resit external assessments during their programme, and Pearson directs centres to its website for the current policy on permitted retakes. The specification says learners may resit to obtain a higher grade of near pass or above, and that the best result is used, up to the permitted maximum.
  • Improving a grade after certification. Pearson's specification says a centre must first decline the learner's overall qualification grade before a resit to improve it.

Pearson's Centre Guide to internal assessment makes the same point for students who top up a smaller BTEC qualification: externally assessed units can be retaken, but not beyond the maximum number of attempts allowed for the qualification. Ask your centre how many attempts you have left and when the next sittings are.

Access Arrangements, Special Consideration and Malpractice

  • Access arrangements. Agreed with Pearson before the assessment, for students with special educational needs, disabilities or temporary injuries. Pearson says they should be processed at the time of registration.
  • Special consideration. An adjustment to a mark or grade made after an external assessment, for temporary injury, illness or other indisposition at the time. Your centre must notify Pearson promptly. It is made only if the impact is reasonably likely to have had a material effect.
  • Malpractice. For external assessment, centres follow the JCQ procedures for suspected malpractice. Heads of centre must report suspected learner malpractice to Pearson, and Pearson decides the sanctions.

How do you prepare differently for an external unit? Because your tutor does not mark it, you cannot use feedback on a draft to improve it, so practising the question style and timing before the sitting matters more.

Are all BTEC units externally assessed?

No. Which units are external depends on your qualification and its specification year. In Pearson's 2025 Construction and the Built Environment Extended Certificate, for example, two of the five units studied are external, making up 50% of the qualification. Check the assessment table in your specification.

Can I resit a failed BTEC external exam?

Yes, but as a resit, not a resubmission. Pearson says its external assessments are available twice a year. The number of attempts depends on your qualification: the 2025 Nationals allow three in total, and for earlier ones Pearson's current retake policy applies, so ask your centre.

How do I know whether one of my units is externally assessed?

Check the unit list and assessment table in your qualification specification, or the assessment plan your centre gives you. Unit titles and which units are external change between specification years, so do not rely on an older unit list.

Is external assessment harder than internal assessment?

It isn't inherently harder, but it demands different skills: timed exam technique and command-word precision, rather than sustained coursework development over several weeks.
