This page covers Pearson's BTEC Level 3 National in Information Technology (2016), the qualification many colleges teach as BTEC IT. It sets out the five sizes, which units are Pearson-set exams or tasks and which your centre marks, and how to approach each kind. It follows Pearson's specification (Issue 6, April 2021). The Higher National computing qualifications are separate. Pearson also lists a 2025 range of qualifications with different units, so check your registration first.

BTEC National IT: Sizes and Structure

The specification covers five sizes. The Extended Diploma and Diploma carry UCAS tariff points.

So this National is not assessed entirely by coursework. Every size includes at least one externally assessed unit. Pearson sets and marks those. Your centre marks the internal units, and Pearson checks the centre's work through external standards verification.

The Mandatory Units in the Extended Diploma

The Extended Diploma has seven mandatory units, four external and three internal. Learners must achieve at least a Near Pass in each mandatory external unit and a Pass or above in each mandatory internal unit. Smaller sizes use fewer of these units, so check the structure for your own size in the specification.

The Four External Units

  • Unit 1: Information Technology Systems. A written examination set and marked by Pearson, lasting two hours and worth 90 marks. It tests understanding of computer systems and the implications of their use in personal and professional situations. The content areas are digital devices in IT systems, transmitting data, operating online, and protecting data and information. It is available in January and May/June.
  • Unit 2: Creating Systems to Manage Information. A task set and marked by Pearson, completed on a computer under supervised conditions and worth 66 marks. Part A is three hours on the afternoon of the first day and Part B is two hours on the morning of the second. It assesses your ability to design, create, test and evaluate a relational database system. The content covers relational database structure and design methods and evaluating a database development project.
  • Unit 11: Cyber Security and Incident Management. A task set and marked by Pearson, worth 80 marks and marked with a levels-based mark scheme. Part A is five hours and Part B is four hours, both within a three-week assessment period that Pearson sets. It assesses your ability to design cyber security measures for networked systems and to analyse a security incident.
  • Unit 14: IT Service Delivery. A task set and marked by Pearson, worth 68 marks. Part A is up to three hours of preparatory research in a two-week period before Part B, and Part B is eight hours within the three-week assessment period. It assesses your ability to analyse an organisation's aims and IT service needs and design an IT service solution. This is the qualification's synoptic assessment, so it draws on learning from across your programme.

The task units are available in December/January and May/June each year. Pearson says learners may resit external assessments during their programme, and its current retake policy applies, so ask your centre.

The Internal Units

For internal units, read the learning aims in the specification and match each task in your Assignment Brief to the criteria it targets.

  • Unit 3: Using Social Media in Business. Exploring the impact of social media on how businesses promote their products and services, developing a plan to use social media in a business, and implementing it.
  • Unit 4: Programming. Examining computational thinking skills and the principles of computer programming, designing a software solution to meet client requirements, and developing it. The unit content gives examples of procedural languages such as C and Python, object-orientated languages such as C++, C# and Java, an event-driven language, Visual Basic, and mark-up such as HTML. It also covers testing, including white box and black box testing. Which language you use is set by your centre and brief.
  • Unit 9: IT Project Management. Investigating the principles and methodologies of IT project management as used in industry, carrying out a project initiation, and carrying out the planning, execution, monitoring and controlling of an IT project.

Internal units are graded Pass, Merit or Distinction. For how those criteria work, see BTEC grading criteria explained.

What separates Merit from Distinction in an IT assignment? The answer is in the criteria your brief targets. Read each command verb, such as explain, analyse or evaluate, and check that every part of your answer, including any working solution and its test evidence, does what that verb asks for.

Progression and Registration Dates

Pearson says the Extended Diploma fully meets entry requirements for progression to a degree course in IT and related areas, such as digital technology solutions, IT management for business, computer networks security, or business computing and entrepreneurship. It says the Diploma meets entry requirements in its own right for some courses in IT or related study, such as an HNC or HND in Computing, Engineering or Business Management, and that for a degree course learners should normally study it alongside other qualifications. BTEC Nationals attract UCAS points, and the amount depends on your grade and qualification size.

Pearson's qualification page lists last-registration dates that differ by size, funding route and nation. When we checked in November 2026, DfE-funded registrations in England showed 31 July 2025 for the Certificate, Extended Certificate and Foundation Diploma, a date that has passed, and 31 December 2026 for private or apprenticeship-funded registrations in England. The Diploma showed 31 July 2028 and the Extended Diploma 31 July 2027 for England, and Northern Ireland and Wales showed 31 July 2029 for all five sizes. Check with your centre which qualification you are registered on.

Does BTEC National IT have an external examination?

Yes. In Pearson's 2016 National, Unit 1 Information Technology Systems is a two-hour written exam worth 90 marks, and Units 2, 11 and 14 are Pearson-set supervised tasks done on a computer. Every size includes at least one external unit, and the Extended Diploma has four. The internal units are marked by your centre and subject to external standards verification.

What coding language is used in BTEC IT programming assignments?

Pearson does not require one language. The Unit 4 content gives examples of procedural languages such as C and Python, object-orientated languages such as C++, C# and Java, an event-driven language, Visual Basic, and mark-up such as HTML. Your centre and Assignment Brief decide which language you use, so follow the brief.

How do I show test evidence for a BTEC IT programming assignment?

Unit 4 covers testing software solutions, and its content names methods such as white box and black box testing, refinement and optimisation. Keep a record of what you tested, the data you used, what you expected, what happened and what you changed, and match it to the criterion in your brief.

Can BTEC National IT get me into a computing degree?

Pearson says the Extended Diploma fully meets entry requirements for degree courses in IT and related areas, and that the Diploma meets entry requirements in its own right for some courses, though for a degree it should normally be studied alongside other qualifications. Each university sets its own requirements, so check the courses you want.
