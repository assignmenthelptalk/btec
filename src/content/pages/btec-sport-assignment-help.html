This page covers Pearson's BTEC Level 3 National in Sport (2016), the qualification many colleges teach as BTEC Sport. It sets out the sizes, which units are Pearson-set exams or tasks and which your centre marks, and how to approach each kind. It follows Pearson's specification (Issue 9, April 2021). Pearson lists Sport and Sport and Exercise Science as separate Level 3 National suites, and Sport and Outdoor Activities (2019) as another, so check the title on your registration. The Higher National qualifications are separate too.

BTEC National Sport: Sizes and Structure

The specification covers five sizes in Sport, plus a Diploma in Fitness Services of 720 GLH that is designed for entry to roles such as gym instructor or fitness instructor.

So this National is not assessed entirely by coursework. Every size includes at least one externally assessed unit. Pearson sets and marks those. Your centre marks the internal units, and Pearson checks the centre's work through external standards verification.

The Mandatory Units in the Extended Diploma

The Extended Diploma has ten mandatory units, four external and six internal. Learners must achieve at least a Near Pass in each mandatory external unit and a Pass or above in each group A internal unit. They must also complete every group B unit and achieve at least a Pass in three of them. Smaller sizes use fewer of these units, so check the structure for your own size in the specification.

The Four External Units

  • Unit 1: Anatomy and Physiology. A written examination set and marked by Pearson, lasting one hour 30 minutes and worth 80 marks, with short- and long-answer questions. It covers the skeletal, muscular, respiratory and cardiovascular systems and the energy systems for sports performance, and asks you to use that knowledge to work out how the body systems relate to each other in sports performance. It is available in January and May/June.
  • Unit 2: Fitness Training and Programming for Health, Sport and Well-being. A task set and marked by Pearson under supervised conditions. Learners get a case study one week before a supervised period of at most 2.5 hours. The task asks you to interpret lifestyle factors and health screening data from a scenario and develop and justify a fitness training programme and nutritional advice. It is available in December/January and May/June.
  • Unit 19: Development and Provision of Sport and Physical Activity. A set task worth 60 marks, with a case study given one week before a supervised period of at most 2.5 hours. It assesses your ability to plan and develop a sports development proposal for a given scenario. It is available in January and May/June.
  • Unit 22: Investigating Business in the Sport and Active Leisure Industry. A set task worth 64 marks, with information given two weeks before a supervised period of at most three hours. It assesses your ability to examine the internal and external factors affecting a business and how it may respond to trends in the industry. It is available in January and May/June.

Pearson says learners may resit an external assessment to obtain a higher grade of Near Pass or above, and that where there is more than one attempt the best result is used, up to the permitted maximum.

The Internal Units

For internal units, read the learning aims in the specification and match each task in your Assignment Brief to the criteria it targets.

  • Unit 3: Professional Development in the Sports Industry. Understanding career and job opportunities, exploring your own skills using a skills audit to inform a career development action plan, undertaking a recruitment activity, and reflecting on the recruitment and selection process and your performance.
  • Unit 4: Sports Leadership. The roles, qualities and characteristics of an effective sports leader, the psychological factors linked to effective leadership, and exploring an effective leadership style when leading a team.
  • Unit 7: Practical Sports Performance. National Governing Body rules and regulations for selected competitions, the skills, techniques and tactics needed in selected sports, developing your own, and reflecting on your practical performance.
  • Unit 8: Coaching for Performance. The skills, knowledge and qualities of performance coaches, practices used to develop skills, techniques and tactics, demonstrating effective planning of coaching, and exploring its impact.
  • Unit 9: Research Methods in Sport. The importance of research in sporting environments, key issues affecting the quality of research, and applying appropriate research methods to a selected research problem.

Internal units are graded Pass, Merit or Distinction. For how those criteria work, see BTEC grading criteria explained.

What separates Merit from Distinction in a Sport assignment? The answer is in the criteria your brief targets. Read each command verb, such as explain, analyse or evaluate, and check that every part of your answer, including any programme, plan or performance evidence, does what that verb asks for.

Progression and Registration Dates

Pearson says the Foundation Diploma supports progression to an apprenticeship in the sport sector or to a further year of study at Level 3. It describes the Diploma as an Applied General qualification aimed at learners looking to progress to higher education in the sector, and the Extended Diploma as a two-year, full-time course that meets entry requirements in its own right for learners who want to progress to higher education courses in sport before entering employment. BTEC Nationals attract UCAS points, and the amount depends on your grade and qualification size.

Pearson's qualification page lists the same last-registration dates for all five sizes. When we checked in November 2026, DfE-funded registrations in England showed 31 July 2028, and other registrations in England, Northern Ireland and Wales showed 31 July 2029. Check with your centre which qualification you are registered on.

Is the BTEC Sport anatomy and physiology unit externally examined?

Yes. In Pearson's 2016 National, Unit 1 Anatomy and Physiology is a written exam set and marked by Pearson, lasting one hour 30 minutes and worth 80 marks. It is not the only external unit: Units 2, 19 and 22 in the Extended Diploma are Pearson-set supervised tasks. Every size includes at least one external unit, and the internal units are marked by your centre.

What energy system knowledge is required for BTEC Sport assignments?

Unit 1 content area E covers the effects of exercise and sports performance on the energy systems, including the role of ATP and the ATP-PC (alactic) system. Use the content list in the specification for the full detail, and in internal units follow the criteria in your Assignment Brief.

How should a BTEC Sport training programme be structured to achieve Distinction?

In Unit 2, a Pearson-set task, you interpret lifestyle factors and health screening data from a scenario and develop and justify a fitness training programme and nutritional advice. The grade depends on the mark scheme, so use the sample assessment material to see what is rewarded, and make sure every choice in your programme is tied to the client's data.

Is BTEC Sport the same as BTEC Sport and Exercise Science?

No. Pearson lists Sport and Sport and Exercise Science as separate Level 3 National suites, and Sport and Outdoor Activities (2019) as another. The unit titles and external assessments differ, so check the title and number on your registration before using a unit list.
