BTEC Travel and Tourism Unit Structure

Units typically cover UK and worldwide travel destinations, the structure of the travel and tourism sector, customer service in travel and tourism, and tour operations and marketing. The sector-structure units in particular cover public, private, and voluntary sector organisations, from tourist boards to tour operators to transport providers.

BTEC Travel and Tourism Assignment Formats: Destination Research and Sector Analysis

Common evidence types include destination research reports, sector structure analysis, customer service case studies, and marketing campaign proposals. Destination research assignments typically require analysing factors such as accessibility, attractions, infrastructure, and target traveller demographics rather than simply describing a place.

What Distinction Requires in BTEC Travel and Tourism Assignments

Distinction-level work evaluates a destination's appeal against specific traveller needs and current market trends, with a justified conclusion, rather than presenting a single destination or company in isolation. This evaluative step, weighing evidence against a stated audience, is what separates Distinction from a well-researched Pass.

BTEC Travel and Tourism Units and Assessment: What Pearson's Specification Says

This section describes the BTEC Level 3 National Extended Diploma in Travel and Tourism, first taught from September 2019. Other sizes in the same suite have different mandatory units, so check which qualification you are studying.

  • There are eight mandatory units, six internal and two external, and learners complete at least five optional units. External assessment makes up 19% of the total GLH.
  • The mandatory units are Unit 1 The World of Travel and Tourism, Unit 2 Global Destinations, Unit 3 Principles of Marketing in Travel and Tourism, Unit 4 Managing the Customer Experience in Travel and Tourism, Unit 5 Travel and Tourism Enterprises, Unit 16 Researching Current Travel Trends and Key Issues in Travel and Tourism, Unit 17 Managing Accommodation Services and Unit 18 The UK as a Tourist Destination.
  • Optional units include Specialist Tourism, Sustainable Tourism, The Airport Experience, Visitor Attractions, Passenger Transport and The Cruise Industry.
  • Unit 1 is a written examination set and marked by Pearson. It lasts 1.5 hours and is worth 75 marks. It is about the scope of the industry and using statistics to analyse trends.
  • Unit 2 is a task set and marked by Pearson, completed under supervised conditions. You are given information two weeks beforehand to research, then complete the task in a single session of three hours. It is worth 60 marks. The unit covers the features and appeal of global destinations, travel planning, and the factors and trends affecting how popular destinations are.
  • Unit 16 is internally assessed and is the synoptic unit. You develop a research proposal on a current travel trend and a key issue, drawing on learning from across your programme.

Units 1 and 2 are taken by you under Pearson's conditions, so the guidance on this page about assignments applies to the internally assessed units. See BTEC external assessment explained for how external assessments work.

Source: Pearson's BTEC Level 3 National Extended Diploma in Travel and Tourism specification (issue 6, September 2021). Pearson revises specifications, so check the current issue.

Reading Travel and Tourism Criteria: The Marketing Unit as a Worked Example

Unit 3, Principles of Marketing in Travel and Tourism, shows how the three grade levels build on each other. Its learning aims ask for different kinds of evidence.

  • Learning aim A, the role of marketing. At Pass you explain how effective marketing and customer service work together to influence customer decisions and meet customer needs, and the impact of the marketing mix. At Merit you analyse those impacts. At Distinction you evaluate them.
  • Learning aim B, two organisations. You explain how two travel and tourism organisations conduct marketing differently to achieve their objectives, taking account of internal and external factors. At Distinction you evaluate the impact of the different approaches on organisational success and justify recommendations for improvement.
  • Learning aim C, market research. You identify a new travel and tourism product or service using data from primary and secondary market research. At Pass you use the data, at Merit you analyse it, and at Distinction you justify the data you obtained.

Two practical points follow from the wording. In learning aim B the Distinction criterion names recommendations for improvement, so a comparison of two organisations that stops at description cannot reach it. In learning aim C, collecting data is not enough: the criteria ask what the data shows and why it is a sound basis for your product or service idea.

What levels is BTEC Travel and Tourism available at?

Primarily BTEC National, Level 3. It's less commonly offered at HNC or HND compared to subjects like Business or Health and Social Care.

What does a BTEC Travel and Tourism destination research assignment involve?

Analysing a destination's appeal against factors such as accessibility, attractions, infrastructure, and target traveller demographics, usually presented as a structured report.

Is BTEC Travel and Tourism similar to BTEC Business?

There's meaningful overlap in marketing and customer service units, but Travel and Tourism focuses specifically on the sector's structure, destinations, and traveller behaviour.

What careers does BTEC Travel and Tourism lead to?

Common progression routes include tourism management, travel operations, hospitality, and further study in tourism or business-related degrees.
