This page covers Pearson's BTEC Level 3 National in Uniformed Protective Services (2020), the qualification many colleges teach under the name BTEC Public Services. It sets out the sizes and units in Pearson's specification, which two units are exams and how they are run, and how to approach the coursework units. If you are on the earlier Public Services (2010) suite, your units and criteria differ, so check your registration first. Our page on Uniformed vs Public Services explains how to do that.

BTEC National Uniformed Protective Services: Sizes and Structure

Pearson's specification (Issue 4, July 2022) lists five qualifications, first taught from January 2020. Pearson defines the uniformed protective services as the armed services, police, fire and rescue service and custodial services.

So this National is not assessed entirely by coursework. Each size includes at least one externally assessed unit. Your centre marks the internal units, and they are subject to external standards verification by Pearson. Pearson sets and marks the external units.

The Mandatory Units

In the Extended Diploma there are seven mandatory units, five internal (one of which is Pearson-set) and two external. Learners must achieve at least a Near Pass in the mandatory external units and a Pass or above in the mandatory internal units.

The Two Exam Units

  • Unit 2: Behaviour and Discipline. A written examination set and marked by Pearson. It lasts two hours and carries 80 marks. The unit looks at factors that affect behaviour and discipline, the theories behind behaviour and how authority is used, and the specification says you can draw on learning from across the programme.
  • Unit 6: Government and the Protective Services. A written examination set and marked by Pearson, lasting one and a half hours and carrying 70 marks. The unit covers the scope of protective services, statutory requirements at different levels of government, how services are funded and held to account, and how government policy is developed and affects services.

Both are available in January and May/June each year. The first sitting was May/June 2021 for Unit 2 and January 2022 for Unit 6. Resit rules depend on the qualification; see BTEC external assessment explained.

The Coursework Units

For internal units, read the learning aims in the specification and match each task in your Assignment Brief to the criteria it targets. Our guide to reading an assignment brief shows how.

  • Unit 1: Citizenship and Diversity (Pearson-set). Your centre sets the assignment brief, based on a theme and topics that Pearson releases and changes each year. Learners research, interpret and analyse information on a current aspect of citizenship and diversity in a protective services context. The four learning aims are the key features of society, rights and responsibilities, the role of protective services in ensuring equality of service, and changes in society that affect citizens and the services.
  • Unit 4: Physical Preparation, Health and Wellbeing. The learning aims are components of fitness and the effect of training on body systems, lifestyle factors in health and wellbeing, carrying out tests for personal fitness and the fitness requirements for entry to the services, and designing a fitness programme for entry goals. Pearson notes that different services have different fitness tests, so check the tests your own brief names and do not assume one standard.
  • Unit 5: Teamwork, Leadership and Communication. The learning aims cover leadership styles, theories and techniques for building effective teams, communication methods and systems used by the services, and applying team working and leadership skills in services contexts.
  • Unit 7: Planning for and Responding to Emergency Incidents. Learners examine emergency incidents and the roles of the services, plan a response to a simulated incident and implement it, and compare their plan with a real incident.

For how Pass, Merit and Distinction criteria work, see BTEC grading criteria explained. The command verb in each criterion tells you the depth required, and the exact wording is in your specification, not on a general web page.

What separates Merit from Distinction in a Public Services assignment? The answer is in the criteria your brief targets. Read each command verb, such as explain, analyse or evaluate, and check that every part of your answer does what that verb asks for.

Progression from BTEC National Uniformed Protective Services

Pearson says the Extended Diploma is designed for learners who want to focus on the uniformed protective services with the aim of progressing to higher education to a related degree, and that it supports progression to higher education in its own right. The Diploma supports progression to higher education when taken with other BTEC Nationals or A levels, and to employment in the uniformed protective services. The units name degree areas: Unit 2 points to courses in policing, fire and rescue or offender management, Unit 6 to public-sector-related study, and Unit 7 to programmes in policing, police and criminal justice studies and public service management.

BTEC Nationals attract UCAS points, and the amount depends on your grade and qualification size; see BTEC UCAS points. Entry to services such as the police, fire and rescue and the armed forces is run by those organisations, which set their own selection processes and requirements, so check each service's current recruitment page.

Does BTEC National Public Services have any external examinations?

Yes. In Pearson's 2020 Uniformed Protective Services suite, Unit 2 Behaviour and Discipline (two hours, 80 marks) and Unit 6 Government and the Protective Services (one and a half hours, 70 marks) are written exams set and marked by Pearson, and every qualification size includes at least one external unit. The internal units are marked by your centre and subject to external standards verification. If you are on the earlier Public Services suite, check your own specification.

What legislation is most important for BTEC Public Services assignments?

Key legislation varies by unit, but across the qualification students most commonly need: Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 (PACE), police powers, arrest, custody, evidence; Equality Act 2010, protected characteristics, public sector equality duty; Human Rights Act 1998, rights in the European Convention; Children Act 1989 and 2004, safeguarding; Crime and Disorder Act 1998, partnership working; Health and Safety at Work Act 1974, operational risk management. The Assignment Brief for each unit will specify the legislation relevant to that assessment.

How do I write at Distinction level for a government and politics unit in BTEC Public Services?

Distinction in government and politics units requires evaluating policy effectiveness, not just describing policy content. Use evidence from official reports: National Audit Office reports, HMICFRS inspection findings, parliamentary select committee reports, ONS statistics, to make a justified assessment of whether a policy has achieved its stated objectives. Identify tensions: between central government direction and local service operational independence, between austerity-driven resource constraints and service demand, between political objectives and professional public service values. Reach a justified conclusion with a defended position.

Which criminological theories are most important for BTEC Public Services crime units?

Core criminological theories for BTEC Public Services crime units include strain theory (Merton), subcultural theory (Cohen), labelling theory (Becker), social control theory (Hirschi), broken windows theory (Wilson and Kelling), left realism (Lea and Young), and social learning theory (Sutherland's differential association). At Merit level, apply the theory that best explains the crime type being analysed. At Distinction level, evaluate competing theories, identifying the strengths and limitations of each and reaching a justified position on which provides the most convincing explanation, with reference to empirical evidence.
