This page covers Pearson's BTEC Level 3 National in Health and Social Care (2016), the qualification many colleges teach as BTEC Health and Social Care. It sets out the five sizes, which units are Pearson-set exams and which your centre marks, and how to approach each kind. It follows Pearson's specification (Issue 8, October 2019). Pearson also lists 2025 versions of this subject with their own units, so check your registration first. Our subjects list shows both generations.

BTEC National Health and Social Care: Sizes and Structure

The specification covers five sizes, all first taught from September 2016, plus a Health Studies pathway of the Extended Diploma for learners aiming at a health-related degree.

The Mandatory Units in the Extended Diploma

The Extended Diploma has eight mandatory units, four external and four internal. Learners must achieve at least a Near Pass in each mandatory external unit and a Pass or above in each mandatory internal unit. Smaller sizes use fewer of these units, so check the structure for your own size in the specification.

Extended Diploma learners also complete at least five optional units, two from group A, two from group B and one more from either. Examples include Infection Prevention and Control, Psychological Perspectives, Caring for Individuals with Dementia, Nutritional Health and Understanding Mental Wellbeing in group A, and Sociological Perspectives, Supporting Individuals with Additional Needs and Policy in Health and Social Care in group B. Which options you study depends on your centre.

The Four External Units

  • Unit 1: Human Lifespan Development. One written examination of 90 marks lasting 1 hour 30 minutes, with short- and long-answer questions that use contexts and data. The content areas are human growth and development through the life stages, the factors affecting growth and development, and the effects of ageing.
  • Unit 2: Working in Health and Social Care. One written examination of 80 marks lasting 1 hour 30 minutes, with short- and long-answer questions. The paper has four sections, each based on a different short scenario. The content areas are the roles and responsibilities of people who work in the sector, the roles of organisations, and working with people with specific needs.
  • Unit 3: Anatomy and Physiology for Health and Social Care. One written examination of 90 marks lasting 1 hour 30 minutes, with short- and long-answer and multiple-choice questions. The content areas are the structure and organisation of the human body, the structure, function and disorders of body systems, and medical research.
  • Unit 4: Enquiries into Current Research in Health and Social Care. A task set and marked by Pearson under supervised conditions, worth 65 marks. Learners receive a research article (Part A) four weeks before the supervised assessment and compile notes on their secondary research in monitored sessions of six hours that the centre schedules. Part B is a three-hour supervised session in a single morning. This is the unit's synoptic assessment, so it draws on learning from across your programme.

External assessments are available twice a year, and Pearson says learners may resit them during their programme. Pearson's current retake policy applies, so ask your centre. See BTEC external assessment explained for how the generations differ. Pearson publishes sample assessment material, and papers and mark schemes from earlier series are available to teachers and students on its website.

The Four Internal Units

For internal units, read the learning aims in the specification and match each task in your Assignment Brief to the criteria it targets. Our guide to reading an assignment brief shows how.

  • Unit 5: Meeting Individual Care and Support Needs. The learning aims are the principles, values and skills that underpin meeting care and support needs, the ethical issues involved, the principles behind enabling individuals to overcome challenges, and the roles of professionals and how they work together.
  • Unit 6: Work Experience in Health and Social Care. Learners examine the benefits of work experience, develop a work experience plan, carry out work experience tasks to meet set objectives, and reflect on how the experience influences their personal and professional development.
  • Unit 7: Principles of Safe Practice in Health and Social Care. The learning aims are how a duty of care contributes to safe practice, recognising and responding to concerns about abuse and neglect, the influence of health and safety legislation and policies, and procedures and responsibilities for health and safety and for accidents and emergencies.
  • Unit 8: Promoting Public Health. Learners examine strategies for developing public health policy, the factors affecting health, how health is promoted, and how health promotion encourages individuals to change their behaviour.

Internal units are graded Pass, Merit or Distinction. For how those criteria work, see BTEC grading criteria explained. The command verb in each criterion tells you the depth required, and the exact wording is in your specification, not on a general web page. If an internal unit falls short, see our resubmission guide.

What separates Merit from Distinction in a Health and Social Care assignment? The answer is in the criteria your brief targets. Read each command verb, such as explain, analyse or evaluate, and check that every part of your answer, including any service user scenario, does what that verb asks for.

Progression and Registration Dates

Pearson says the Extended Diploma supports progression to health and social care related degrees, and the Foundation Diploma supports progression to an Apprenticeship in the sector or to a further year of study at Level 3. The Extended Certificate supports progression to higher education when taken as part of a programme that includes other appropriate BTEC Nationals or A levels. BTEC Nationals attract UCAS points, and the amount depends on your grade and qualification size; see BTEC UCAS points.

Pearson's qualification page lists last-registration dates that differ by size, funding route and nation. When we checked in November 2026, the Certificate, Extended Certificate and Foundation Diploma showed 31 July 2025 for DfE-funded registrations in England, the Diploma and Extended Diploma showed 31 July 2028, and Northern Ireland showed 31 July 2029. Check with your centre which qualification you are registered on.

What theories are most important in BTEC Health and Social Care assignments?

It depends on the unit. Pearson's specification names theorists across its units, including Piaget, Vygotsky, Bowlby, Erikson, Maslow and Bandura, and Unit 1 asks you to relate theories and models of development to the stages of life. The theories you need are the ones your Assignment Brief and unit specification point to, so start there rather than from a general list.

Does BTEC Health and Social Care have any external examinations?

Yes. In Pearson's 2016 National, Units 1, 2 and 3 are written exams set and marked by Pearson (90, 80 and 90 marks, each 1 hour 30 minutes), and Unit 4 is a Pearson-set supervised task worth 65 marks. Every size includes at least one external unit, and the Extended Diploma has four. The internal units are marked by your centre and subject to external standards verification.

What legislation is most important to know for BTEC H&SC safeguarding assignments?

The specification does not give one fixed list. Unit 7 covers duty of care, recognising and responding to concerns about abuse and neglect, and health and safety legislation, and Unit 5 lists legislation such as the Mental Health Act 2007, Human Rights Act 1998, Mental Capacity Act 2005, Equality Act 2010 and Care Act 2014 as examples. Use the legislation your brief and unit content name, and check which nation's law applies at your centre.

How detailed does a reflective account need to be at BTEC National level?

Pearson does not set a length or prescribe a reflective model. In Unit 6, learning aim D asks you to reflect on how work experience influences your personal and professional development, and the depth expected is set by the verbs in your criteria. Use the model your centre teaches, cover every part of the criterion, and link your reflection to what you actually did and learned.
