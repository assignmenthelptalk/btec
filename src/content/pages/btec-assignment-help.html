BTEC assignment help covers Pearson's BTEC Level 3 Nationals and its Higher Nationals, the Level 4 HNC and Level 5 HND, across the subjects Pearson offers. Internally assessed BTEC units are assessed through an assignment brief that sets out Pass, Merit and Distinction criteria. This page explains how the qualifications are structured and graded, who marks what, what Pearson's rules say about resubmission, and how our service works. The facts follow Pearson's published specifications and its Centre Guide to internal assessment (version 4.1). Rules differ between qualification generations, so confirm yours with your centre.

How BTEC Grading Works

For internally assessed units, your assignment brief lists criteria labelled P, M and D. Pearson's rule for the unit grade is that you achieve the highest level for which you have met all the criteria: to achieve a Distinction you must satisfy all the Distinction criteria and all the Pass and Merit criteria, and to achieve a Merit you must satisfy all the Merit criteria and all the Pass criteria. Pearson says a Pass is a defined level of performance and cannot be given just because you completed the assignment. A learner who does not satisfy the Pass criteria is reported as Unclassified.

Each criterion starts with a command verb that sets the type of response. In one Higher National Business unit, for example, the grid pairs a Pass criterion that asks you to explain with a Merit criterion that asks you to analyse and a Distinction criterion that asks you to critically analyse. Match every part of your answer to the verb for the criterion it targets. Our guide to BTEC grading criteria goes through this in detail.

Externally assessed National units work differently. Pearson marks them and awards Distinction, Merit, Pass or Near Pass, and Near Pass is used for external units only. The overall National grade is an aggregate: every unit contributes in proportion to its guided learning hours, so a 120-hour unit counts double a 60-hour unit, and Pearson says the aggregate includes some compensation, because a higher result in one unit can balance a lower one in another. Nationals are graded from Pass to Distinction*, with Distinction* awarded for the whole qualification and not for a single unit. Higher Nationals are graded Pass, Merit and Distinction.

Who Marks What: Internal and External Assessment

Internal units. Your centre sets the assignment brief, or uses a Pearson authorised brief, and your tutor judges your evidence against the criteria. A different member of staff at the centre internally verifies a sample of that marking, and Pearson's standards verifiers later sample the centre's work. For the BTEC Nationals from 2025, Pearson writes the assignment briefs and your centre marks them. See how BTEC internal assessment works.

External units. Pearson sets and marks these. In the 2016 Nationals they are written exams or tasks completed under supervised conditions, scheduled on fixed dates. Every size of every 2016 National we have checked includes at least one external unit, but the share varies. In the Extended Diploma it is 42% for Business, Health and Social Care, IT and Sport, and 33% in the Childcare Extended Diploma (Early Years Educator). Business Unit 3, Personal and Business Finance, is a two-hour exam. Business Unit 2, Developing a Marketing Campaign, and Unit 6, Principles of Management, are Pearson-set tasks. See BTEC external assessment explained.

Higher Nationals. Pearson describes internal assignments that centres set and assess, and Pearson-set assignments that centres set in line with Pearson's guidelines and grade. The specification we checked does not describe Pearson-set exams.

BTEC Resubmission and Retake Rules

Pearson's Centre Guide to internal assessment (version 4.1) sets these rules for internally assessed units on BTEC Level 3 Nationals. The word "referral" is common in college handbooks, but Pearson's National specifications record a unit that has not reached a Pass as Unclassified.

  • One resubmission per assignment. A student may be eligible, but it is not automatic. The Lead Internal Verifier can authorise it only if you met the original deadline or an agreed extension, the assessor judges that you can produce improved evidence without further guidance, and you have completed a declaration of authentication.
  • A deadline. An authorised resubmission is recorded on the assessment record and carries a deadline within 15 working days of you receiving your results, in term time and in the same academic year.
  • Not capped at a Pass. Pearson says you should be able to submit to the full range of criteria the assignment targets, and you need to resubmit only the criteria identified as needing further work.
  • One retake, in exceptional circumstances. If you met the conditions and still have not achieved the targeted Pass criteria, the Lead Internal Verifier may authorise a single retake. After a retake there are no further resubmissions or retakes.
  • No resubmission without the deadline. A student who missed the original deadline, without an agreed extension, has neither a resubmission nor a retake.

External units are resat, not resubmitted. The number of attempts depends on the qualification, so ask your centre. Higher Nationals have their own rule: Pearson says a student whose assignment does not pass must have the opportunity to resubmit it once, capped at a Pass, and may be allowed to repeat the unit once. Why Students Lose Marks on BTEC Assignments

Pearson's published Lead Examiner reports for external units record marks lost for not answering the command verb and for ignoring the marks available. The same discipline helps in coursework. A criterion that asks you to analyse is not met by a description, however accurate, and a criterion that asks you to evaluate is not met by analysis alone. See Pearson BTEC examiner feedback reports.

Completing the task and meeting the criterion are not the same thing, so check each criterion in your brief against a specific part of your submission. Build the answer as one argument that meets Pass, Merit and Distinction criteria in turn, instead of treating each as a separate piece of writing. Use the referencing style your centre or brief names, and cite your sources consistently. External units need a different skill: Pearson's exams and set tasks are timed and marked to a mark scheme, so practise the question style before the sitting.

How Our BTEC Assignment Help Service Works

The service begins when the student submits their Assignment Brief, the document issued by the tutor that specifies the unit title, learning outcomes, task description, and P/M/D criteria. No prior work is required. The Assignment Brief contains everything needed to build the response framework.

Step 1. Submit your Assignment Brief along with your current level (National, HNC, or HND), subject, and submission deadline. If you have a partially completed draft or a submission that did not pass, include that too.

Step 2. An expert reviews the criteria verbs, identifies the evidence required at each grade band, and constructs a response framework that maps explicitly to each P, M, and D criterion in the brief.

Step 3. A model answer or guided structure is delivered showing exactly how evidence maps to each criterion, including the language register required at your level (Level 3, Level 4, or Level 5).

Step 4. Where your centre requires a referencing style, sources are formatted in-text and in the reference list in that style, using appropriate sources for the subject area and grade target.

Step 5. The student reviews the model answer, asks clarifying questions about any criterion, and receives explanation of why each element of the response satisfies the specific criterion it is mapped to.

BTEC vs A-Level: Understanding the Assessment Difference

A-levels and BTEC Nationals both generate UCAS points and are accepted by UK universities, but they are assessed differently. A-levels are mainly assessed by exams. In a BTEC National, many units are marked by your centre against criteria, while others are set and marked by Pearson, and the balance depends on the subject and size.

Under the current UCAS tariff, a BTEC National Extended Diploma graded D*D*D* is worth 168 points, the same as three A* grades at A level. Individual universities set their own entry requirements, so check the course you want. See BTEC UCAS points and BTEC vs A-level.
