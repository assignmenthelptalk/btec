A BTEC National can lead to university, an apprenticeship, a job or self-employment. Pearson's specifications describe all four, and several give subject-specific examples of the degrees and apprenticeships a qualification supports. This page sets out what Pearson says about each route, using its published specifications, so you can see which ones apply to your subject.

University Progression From BTEC

Pearson's specifications state that BTEC Nationals attract UCAS points, and direct students to the UCAS website for the amounts. Pearson's Travel and Tourism specification (September 2021) also says BTEC Nationals are accepted by over 150 UK universities and higher education institutes for relevant degree programmes, either on their own or in combination with A levels. That is Pearson's own statement, so check each course's entry requirements on the university's website.

Subject specifications give concrete examples. Pearson's 2025 Construction and the Built Environment Extended Certificate says it can lead to progression to Architecture BA or BSc, Construction Management BSc and Civil Engineering BEng degrees. It names Mathematics, Physics, Design and Technology and Art and Design as subjects that combine well with it.

For how grades convert to points, see BTEC UCAS points, and for which universities accept BTEC, see universities that accept BTEC.

Apprenticeship Routes From BTEC

Pearson's wording on apprenticeships is specific to each qualification:

  • Pearson's BTEC Nationals in Construction and the Built Environment (2017 specification) say the Foundation Diploma supports progression to an Apprenticeship in the construction sector or to a further year of study at Level 3, and that the Diploma can be used to progress to Higher Apprenticeships.
  • The Diplomas in Building Services Engineering and Civil Engineering can be used, when studied part time, as part of an advanced technician Apprenticeship in the same field, or for progression to a Higher Apprenticeship in it.
  • The creative digital media and art and design specifications describe their specialist National qualifications as giving knowledge and skills that enable entry to an Apprenticeship or other employment, or progression to related higher education.

These are statements about what a qualification supports. Apprenticeship places are filled by employers, who set their own requirements.

Direct Employment and Self-Employment

Pearson's Nationals specifications say the qualifications provide progression to the workplace either directly or by study at a higher level, and tell students their BTEC supports a move into work, an Apprenticeship or setting up their own business. The Travel and Tourism specification adds that its learners are aiming to go on to employment, often via higher education, and links units to roles such as travel sales consultant, event planner and travel and tourism entrepreneur. The same specification cites YouGov research showing 62 per cent of large companies have recruited employees with BTEC qualifications.

Pearson says its specialist National qualifications require significant employer involvement in the programme. Whether a particular job accepts a BTEC on its own is for the employer, so check job listings for your target role.

The Higher National Route: HNC and HND

Pearson describes the Higher Nationals as equivalent to the first and second years of a university degree, giving progression to both university and employment. Whether a university lets Higher National graduates join the final year of a related degree varies by university and course, so ask the university before you rely on it. Our guide to BTEC Higher Nationals explains the HNC and HND.

How to Narrow Down Your Options

  • Read the "who is this qualification for" and "what further learning will this lead to" sections of your specification. Pearson writes these for students.
  • Look at the entry requirements for two or three specific courses or apprenticeships you might want. Requirements vary by provider.
  • Ask your tutor which combination of qualifications your programme is built around, since Pearson designs some Nationals to be taken with A levels or other BTECs.

Want to improve your grade to open more options? Grade requirements are set by each university and employer, and a stronger profile keeps more of them open. Starting from the criteria in your brief is the most direct way to raise a grade.

Grades and Your Options

UCAS points depend on your grade and the size of your qualification, and course entry requirements are set by each university, so a higher grade helps most where entry is competitive. Can you go straight to university with a BTEC?

Yes. Pearson states that BTEC Nationals attract UCAS points, and that over 150 UK universities and higher education institutes accept them for relevant degrees, on their own or with A levels. Each university sets its own entry requirements.

Is BTEC good for getting an apprenticeship?

It can be. Pearson's specifications say some National qualifications support progression to Apprenticeships and Higher Apprenticeships, for example in construction and civil engineering. Employers set their own requirements.

Can you get a job straight after BTEC without going to university?

Pearson's specifications say its Nationals give progression to the workplace, either directly or through higher-level study. Whether a job accepts a BTEC on its own is the employer's decision, so check the requirements in job listings.

Does the grade I get on my BTEC affect my career options?

Yes. UCAS points depend on your grade and the size of your qualification, and universities and employers set their own requirements, so a higher grade keeps more options open.
