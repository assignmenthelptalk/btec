On internally assessed BTEC units, your grade depends on which of the criteria in your Assignment Brief you have met, not on a percentage mark. This page explains how the criterion codes work, what Pearson's own definitions of command verbs such as analyse, assess and evaluate say, how unit grades combine into a qualification grade, and how that differs for Higher Nationals. It follows Pearson's Level 3 National specification (Business, Issue 14, April 2023), its Higher Nationals in Business specification (Issue 3, January 2022) and its Centre Guide to internal assessment (version 4.1). Externally assessed units are marked by Pearson and are covered briefly below.

How BTEC Criterion Codes Work: P, M and D Explained

An Assignment Brief lists the Pass, Merit and Distinction criteria it targets, usually in a grid. Each code has a letter for the grade band and a number for its position in the unit, so P1 and P2 are Pass criteria, M1 is a Merit criterion and D1 a Distinction criterion. The number of criteria in each band varies by unit, so count the ones in your own brief before planning your answer.

Pearson's rule for the unit grade is the highest level for which you have met all the criteria. To achieve a Distinction you must satisfy all the Distinction criteria and all the Pass and Merit criteria, and to achieve a Merit you must satisfy all the Merit criteria and all the Pass criteria. To achieve a Pass you must satisfy all the Pass criteria for the learning aims. Pearson adds that a Pass is a defined level of performance and cannot be given solely because a learner has completed the assignments, and that learners who do not satisfy the Pass criteria are reported as Unclassified.

The practical consequence is that one missing Pass criterion keeps a unit from being passed, however strong your Merit or Distinction writing is. Your centre may call this a referral, but Pearson's National specifications use the term Unclassified. Before you submit, check each criterion in the brief against a specific part of your answer. If you cannot point to the paragraph that meets it, it has not been met. Some criteria are linked, so one extended piece of evidence can meet a Pass, Merit and Distinction criterion on the same learning aim, while others are independent and need their own evidence.

What Pearson Says the Command Verbs Mean

Pearson's National specifications include a glossary of the terms used to define requirements in internally assessed units. These are Pearson's definitions from the Business specification, summarised. Other specifications have their own glossary, and your brief or unit may use a term differently, so use the version for your qualification.

Command verbs in Pearson's glossary for internally assessed units (Business, Issue 14)
TermWhat the glossary says the learner work shows
DescribeA clear, objective account in your own words, showing recall and sometimes application of the relevant features. It normally needs breadth of content.
OutlineA summary, overview or brief description.
IdentifyIndicating the main features or purpose of something by recognising it.
ExplainClear details and reasons or evidence to support an opinion, view or argument. It could show how conclusions are drawn.
AnalyseThe outcome of methodical and detailed examination, either breaking down a theme, topic or situation to study how the parts interrelate, or studying information or data for key trends and interrelationships.
CompareIdentifying the main factors of two or more items or situations and explaining the similarities, differences, advantages and disadvantages.
DiscussConsidering different aspects of a theme or topic, how they interrelate and the extent to which they are important. A conclusion is not required.
AssessCareful consideration of varied factors or events applying to a specific situation, identifying the most important or relevant, and arriving at a conclusion.
JustifyGiving reasons or evidence to support an opinion or to prove something right or reasonable.
EvaluateDrawing on varied information to consider strengths and weaknesses, advantages and disadvantages, alternative actions, and relevance or significance, leading to a supported judgement that relates to its context. This is often in a conclusion.

Pearson does not tie each verb to one grade band. The unit's own criteria do. In the criteria grids we checked, Pass criteria often ask for descriptions or explanations, Merit criteria for analysis or comparison, and Distinction criteria for evaluation. In one Higher National unit, the grid pairs a Pass criterion to explain with a Merit criterion to analyse and a Distinction criterion to critically analyse. The two points to remember are that discuss does not require a conclusion, while assess and evaluate do, and that your answer must do what the verb says for the criterion it targets. How Criterion-Based Grading Differs from Marks

On internal units there is no percentage mark. Each criterion is judged as met or not met, and strength in one criterion does not make up for a missing criterion in the same unit. That is different from an exam, where marks accumulate across a paper.

Externally assessed units work like an exam. Pearson sets and marks them and awards Distinction, Merit, Pass or Near Pass, and Near Pass applies to external units only. It gives credit below the Pass standard for positive performance, and is worth more than an Unclassified result. To be awarded a National qualification, a learner must achieve at least a Near Pass in every external unit and a Pass or above in the mandatory units.

So a National qualification mixes the two approaches, and the combination works at qualification level, as the next section explains. See BTEC external assessment explained.

How Unit Grades Combine Into a Qualification Grade

In Pearson's National specifications, the overall grade is an aggregate of the learner's performance across the qualification. Every unit contributes in proportion to its guided learning hours, so a 120-hour unit counts double a 60-hour unit. Pearson says there is some element of compensation at this level, meaning a higher result in some units can balance a lower one in others. Pearson works out the qualification grade from the unit results, and the minimum points for each grade are set in the specification.

The available grade range depends on the size: Pass to Distinction* for the Certificate, Extended Certificate and Foundation Diploma, PP to D*D* for the Diploma, and PPP to D*D*D* for the Extended Diploma. Distinction* applies to the qualification as a whole and is never awarded on one unit.

UCAS points follow from the size and overall grade. For the Extended Diploma, D*D*D* gives 168 points, DDM 128 and DMM 112. In the Extended Certificate, D* gives 56, D 48, M 32 and P 16. See the BTEC grading system for every combination. Some degree courses specify unit or subject requirements as well as points, so check the course page of each university.

What is the single most important rule in BTEC criteria? A unit grade needs every criterion at its own level and below. Check every criterion in the brief, including the Pass criteria, against the evidence before you submit.

Higher National Grading

Higher Nationals use Pass, Merit and Distinction only, with no Distinction*. Pearson's Business specification gives 4, 6 and 8 points per credit for Pass, Merit and Distinction, and the overall HNC or HND grade is set by points: Pass 420 to 599, Merit 600 to 839 and Distinction 840 and above. For the HND, the overall grade is based on the Level 5 units only.

Pearson says each university sets its own admission criteria for degree entry and that they can change, so use the university's entry requirements for the course you want and not a general grade equivalence table.

Can Distinction-quality writing compensate for a missed Pass criterion in BTEC?

No. Pearson's rule is that a Distinction needs every Distinction criterion and every Pass and Merit criterion to be satisfied. A unit that does not satisfy the Pass criteria is reported as Unclassified, although your centre may call it a referral. Compensation exists only at qualification level, where the overall grade is an aggregate of unit results.

What is the difference between analyse, assess and evaluate in BTEC?

In Pearson's National glossary, analyse is a methodical examination of how parts of a topic interrelate. Assess is a careful consideration of varied factors to identify the most important and arrive at a conclusion. Evaluate considers strengths and weaknesses, advantages and disadvantages and alternative actions, leading to a supported judgement in context. Discuss, by contrast, does not require a conclusion. The unit's criteria decide which verb applies at which grade.

How many criteria does a BTEC unit have?

It varies by unit. The number of Pass, Merit and Distinction criteria is shown in the criteria grid of your Assignment Brief, so count the criteria in your own brief and plan your answer around them.

What does Distinction* mean in BTEC National grading?

Distinction* is a qualification-level grade for BTEC Nationals and is never awarded on a single unit. The overall grade is an aggregate of unit results weighted by guided learning hours, and the minimum points for each grade are in your specification. Higher Nationals are graded Pass, Merit and Distinction only. On the UCAS tariff, D* is worth more than D: in the Extended Certificate, D* gives 56 points and D gives 48.
