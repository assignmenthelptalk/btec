This page covers Pearson's BTEC Level 3 National in Children's Play, Learning and Development (2016), the qualification many colleges teach as BTEC Childcare or Early Years. It sets out the sizes, which units are set and marked by Pearson and which your centre marks, and how to approach each kind. It follows Pearson's specification (Issue 8, March 2022). The qualification is not the same as Health and Social Care, so if your unit list begins with Human Lifespan Development, see our Health and Social Care page instead.

BTEC National Children's Play, Learning and Development: Sizes and Structure

The specification covers four main sizes, first taught from September 2016. The Diploma and Extended Diploma carry the Early Years Educator label, which means they are designed for learners preparing to work as early years educators in preschool and nursery settings. Pearson also lists two alternative versions of the Diploma and Extended Diploma, for learners who do not meet every Early Years Educator requirement. These do not confer Early Years Educator status.

The Mandatory Units in the Extended Diploma

Learners complete eleven mandatory units, three external and eight internal, plus four optional units. Smaller sizes use fewer of these units, so check the structure for your own size in the specification.

The Three External Units

  • Unit 1: Children's Development. A written examination set and marked by Pearson, lasting 1 hour 45 minutes and worth 90 marks. It has scenario-based questions that test applying theory to real-life situations, and long-answer questions that test analysing and interpreting theories. The content covers the principles of growth and development, physical development from birth to seven years 11 months, cognition, language and communication, and theories of social and emotional development.
  • Unit 2: Development of Children's Communication, Literacy and Numeracy Skills. A task set and marked by Pearson under supervised conditions, worth 68 marks. Learners get a task booklet with a case study of a fictional early years setting and complete three activities. They have 3 hours over two days for planning and preparation, then three hours of supervised time to write up the task book.
  • Unit 4: Enquiries into Current Research in Early Years Practice. A task set and marked by Pearson under supervised conditions, worth 65 marks. Learners receive a research article (Part A) four weeks before the supervised assessment and compile notes in monitored sessions of six hours scheduled by the centre. Part B is a three-hour supervised session in a single morning.

External assessments are available once or twice a year, and Pearson says learners may resit them during their programme. Pearson's current retake policy applies, so ask your centre.

The Internal Units

For internal units, read the learning aims in the specification and match each task in your Assignment Brief to the criteria it targets.

  • Unit 3: Play and Learning. Types of play and learning activities and their benefits, theoretical perspectives and curriculum approaches to play, and demonstrating the skills needed to support purposeful play.
  • Unit 5: Keeping Children Safe. Legal responsibilities and health and safety approaches, procedures for preventing and controlling infection, how professionals safeguard children and respond to concerns about abuse, and recognising and assessing hazards and risks.
  • Unit 6: Children's Physical Development, Care and Health Needs. Physical development and care needs, planning and supporting routines and activities, and recognising and responding to children who are unwell.
  • Unit 7: Children's Personal, Social and Emotional Development. Approaches to supporting development and influences on behaviour, preparing and supporting children through transitions and significant life events, and promoting positive behaviour.
  • Unit 8: Working with Parents and Others in Early Years. Partnership work with parents, with colleagues and other professionals, and demonstrating effective partnership work.
  • Unit 9: Observation, Assessment and Planning. The importance of observation, assessment and planning, carrying out and recording observations of children, and creating, implementing and reviewing activity plans. The unit content names methods such as checklists and narrative methods including sticky notes.
  • Unit 10: Reflective Practice. The purpose of reflective practice, theories and approaches to it, and developing reflective practice skills in a setting.
  • Unit 11: The Early Years Foundation Stage. The legal status, principles, themes and aims of the EYFS, its learning and development and assessment requirements, its safeguarding and welfare requirements, and applying skills to support children's progress.

Internal units are graded Pass, Merit or Distinction. For how those criteria work, see BTEC grading criteria explained.

Work placement is a requirement of the qualification. For the Extended Diploma (Early Years Educator), Pearson specifies a minimum of 750 hours in at least two different settings, with at least 100 hours in each of three age ranges: birth to one year 11 months, two years to four years 11 months, and five years to seven years 11 months. The smaller sizes have shorter placement requirements, listed in the specification.

What separates Merit from Distinction in a Childcare assignment? The answer is in the criteria your brief targets. Read each command verb, such as explain, analyse or evaluate, and check that every part of your answer, including any observation or case study, does what that verb asks for.

Progression and Registration Dates

Pearson says the Foundation Diploma supports progression to an Apprenticeship in the early years sector or to a further year of study at Level 3. The Diploma is designed for learners preparing for a role as an early years educator in preschool and nursery settings. The Extended Certificate supports progression to higher education when taken as part of a programme that includes other appropriate BTEC Nationals or A levels. BTEC Nationals attract UCAS points, and the amount depends on your grade and qualification size.

Pearson's qualification page lists last-registration dates by size. When we checked in November 2026, England showed 31 July 2025 for the Extended Certificate and Foundation Diploma and 31 August 2024 for the Diploma and Extended Diploma. Those dates have passed, so these qualifications are for learners already registered. If you are starting now, your centre will be using a newer qualification. Check with your centre which one you are registered on.

Does BTEC National Children's Play, Learning and Development have any external examinations?

Yes. In Pearson's 2016 National, Unit 1 Children's Development is a written exam (90 marks, 1 hour 45 minutes), and Units 2 and 4 are Pearson-set supervised tasks worth 68 and 65 marks. Every size includes at least two external units. The internal units are marked by your centre and subject to external standards verification.

Which child development theories are most important for BTEC CPLD assignments?

It depends on the unit. Pearson's specification names theorists including Piaget, Vygotsky, Bruner, Bowlby, Ainsworth, Skinner, Bandura and Bronfenbrenner, and Unit 1 covers theories of social and emotional development. The theories you need are the ones your Assignment Brief and unit specification point to.

What is the EYFS and why is it important in BTEC Childcare assignments?

The Early Years Foundation Stage is the statutory framework for early years settings, and Unit 11 covers its legal status, principles, learning and development and assessment requirements, and safeguarding and welfare requirements. Several other units draw on it, so use the version of the framework your brief names.

How should a child observation record be written for BTEC CPLD?

Unit 9 asks you to carry out and record observations of children to make accurate assessments, and its content names methods such as checklists and narrative methods, including sticky notes. Use the method your brief asks for, record what you saw rather than your opinion, and follow your setting's confidentiality rules for any child's details.
