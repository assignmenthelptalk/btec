Pearson does not have a BTEC qualification called "Uniformed Public Services". The title on Pearson's current Level 3 National in this area is Uniformed Protective Services, first taught in January 2020. "Public Services" was the name of an earlier Pearson suite. So the two names you have seen may belong to different qualifications, and the one that matters is the title and number on your own registration.

What Does Pearson Call This Qualification?

On Pearson's qualification page, the current suite is listed under the title Uniformed Protective Services (2020). All five qualifications were first taught in January 2020, and the first external assessment was in 2021.

Where "Public Services" and "Uniformed Public Services" Come From

"Public Services" was the title of an earlier Pearson suite of BTEC Nationals, referred to as Public Services (2010). It sits in a different generation of the qualification from the 2020 suite, with its own specification, unit titles and assessment criteria. Pearson's BTEC Nationals page groups the 2020 suite under the heading "Public and Protective Services", which is probably one reason the names get mixed up.

We have not found any Pearson qualification titled "Uniformed Public Services". People use it as shorthand, and a school or college may use its own name for the course on its website or timetable. The name in a prospectus is therefore not a reliable guide to which specification you are following.

How to Confirm Which One You Are On

  • Check the qualification title and number on your registration confirmation, enrolment paperwork or certificate. If the number is one of the five in the table above, you are on the 2020 Uniformed Protective Services suite.
  • Check the header of your Assignment Brief. Briefs normally state the qualification and unit title.
  • Ask your exams officer or tutor which specification your cohort was registered on, and when you started. The registration year decides which version of the units and criteria apply to you.

Do not rely on a unit list, a grading criterion or an example brief from a different suite. Units with similar names can be organised and assessed differently in each generation.

What This Means for Your Assignments

Your tutor assesses your internally assessed work against the unit specification for the suite you are registered on, and Pearson's externally assessed units use papers or tasks written for that suite. Reading the right specification matters more than the course name. Our guides to reading an assignment brief and BTEC grading criteria apply to either suite.

For UCAS, Pearson's specifications state that BTEC Nationals attract UCAS Tariff points, and they point students to the UCAS website for the amounts. Use the exact title on your registration when you apply.

Whichever title your course uses, what do the assignments actually require? The answer comes from the specification and the brief you were given, not from the name on the course page.

Is BTEC Uniformed Public Services a different qualification from BTEC Public Services?

Pearson does not have a qualification titled Uniformed Public Services. Its current Level 3 National in this area is Uniformed Protective Services (2020), and Public Services is the name of an earlier suite with its own specification. Check the title and number on your registration to see which one you are on.

Why do some colleges say "Uniformed Public Services" and others say "Public Services"?

A college can use its own course name, and Public Services is also the name of Pearson's earlier suite. The name in a prospectus does not decide your specification. The qualification title and number on your registration do.

Which title should I use on my UCAS application?

Use the exact title shown on your enrolment paperwork or Assignment Brief, since this is what your centre has formally registered your qualification under.

Does it matter which name my course uses for getting assignment help?

The name matters less than the specification. Give the qualification title and number from your registration when you ask for help, because the units and criteria differ between the 2020 Uniformed Protective Services suite and the earlier Public Services suite.
