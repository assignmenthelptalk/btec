This page covers Pearson's BTEC Level 3 National in Engineering (2016), the qualification many colleges teach as BTEC Engineering. It sets out the sizes, which units are Pearson-set and which your centre marks, and how to approach each kind. It follows Pearson's specification for the Foundation Diploma (Issue 9, June 2022), which contains the shared core units. The Higher National engineering qualifications are separate. Pearson also lists a 2025 range of qualifications with different units, so check your registration first.

BTEC National Engineering: Sizes and Structure

The Certificate, Extended Certificate and Foundation Diploma are general Engineering qualifications. The Diploma and Extended Diploma come in a general Engineering version and in Aeronautical, Electrical and Electronic, Manufacturing and Mechanical versions.

The Extended Diploma is 1080 guided learning hours, equivalent in size to three A levels, and is covered by its own specification. So this National is not assessed entirely by coursework. Every size includes at least one externally assessed unit. Pearson sets and marks those. Your centre marks the internal units, and Pearson checks the centre's work through external standards verification.

The Core Units

Five units are shared by the general Engineering qualifications and the Diploma and Extended Diploma versions. Learners must achieve at least a Near Pass in each mandatory external unit and a Pass or above in each mandatory internal unit.

The Diploma and Extended Diploma versions add further mandatory units that depend on the pathway, such as Microcontroller Systems for Engineers, so check the structure for your own qualification. Optional units include Calculus to Solve Engineering Problems, Further Engineering Mathematics, Work Experience in the Engineering Sector, Computer Aided Design in Engineering, Engineering Maintenance and Condition Monitoring Techniques, Pneumatic and Hydraulic Systems and Welding Technology. Which options you study depends on your centre.

The Two Core External Units

  • Unit 1: Engineering Principles. One paper set and marked by Pearson, lasting two hours and worth 80 marks, with short- and long-answer problem-solving questions set in engineering contexts with data. It focuses on solving problems that need mathematical techniques together with electrical, electronic and mechanical principles. The content areas are algebraic and trigonometric methods, static engineering systems, dynamic engineering systems, fluid engineering systems, static and direct current electricity and circuits, and magnetism and electromagnetic induction. It is available in January and May/June.
  • Unit 3: Engineering Product Design and Manufacture. A set task of 60 marks provided by Pearson and completed under supervised conditions. Learners get a case study one week before the supervised assessment (Part A) and are advised to spend no more than three hours on independent research, with no design work yet. The supervised assessment period is eight hours, arranged over a number of sessions within a two-week period that Pearson sets, and must be completed within five days of starting. Learners follow a standard development process: interpret a brief, scope initial design ideas, prepare a design proposal and evaluate it. It is available in December/January and May/June.

Pearson says learners may resit external assessments during their programme, and its current retake policy applies, so ask your centre.

The Internal Units

For internal units, read the learning aims in the specification and match each task in your Assignment Brief to the criteria it targets.

  • Unit 2: Delivery of Engineering Processes Safely as a Team. Examining common engineering processes to create products or deliver services safely and effectively as a team, developing two-dimensional computer-aided drawings for use in engineering processes, and carrying out engineering processes safely to manufacture a product or deliver a service as a team.
  • Unit 4: Applied Commercial and Quality Principles in Engineering. Examining the business functions and trade considerations that help engineering organisations thrive, exploring activity-based costing to control costs and judge whether a product or service is profitable, and exploring how organisations use quality systems and value management to create value.

Internal units are graded Pass, Merit or Distinction. For how those criteria work, see BTEC grading criteria explained.

What separates Merit from Distinction in an Engineering assignment? The answer is in the criteria your brief targets. Read each command verb, such as explain, analyse or evaluate, and check that every part of your answer, including any calculation, drawing or design justification, does what that verb asks for.

Progression and Registration Dates

Pearson says the Foundation Diploma supports progression to an apprenticeship in engineering or to a further year of study at Level 3, and the Diploma is aimed at learners preparing for roles such as engineering technician or engineering operative. The Extended Certificate supports progression to higher education when taken as part of a programme that includes other appropriate BTEC Nationals or A levels. BTEC Nationals attract UCAS points, and the amount depends on your grade and qualification size.

Pearson's qualification page lists last-registration dates that differ by qualification, funding route and nation. When we checked in November 2026, DfE-funded registrations in England ran from 31 July 2025 to 31 July 2028 depending on the qualification, and the 2025 dates, which apply to the Extended Certificate and to the Diplomas in Electrical and Electronic Engineering and Mechanical Engineering, have passed. Other registrations in England, Northern Ireland and Wales showed 31 July 2030, and the Extended Diploma in Computer Engineering had closed in 2021. Check with your centre which qualification you are registered on.

Does BTEC National Engineering have any external examinations?

Yes. In Pearson's 2016 National, Unit 1 Engineering Principles is a two-hour written paper worth 80 marks and is mandatory at every size, and Unit 3 Engineering Product Design and Manufacture is a Pearson-set supervised task worth 60 marks, mandatory from the Extended Certificate up. The internal units are marked by your centre and subject to external standards verification.

How much working do I need to show in BTEC Engineering calculations?

Unit 1 is built from short- and long-answer problem-solving questions, so show each step of your method as well as the final answer. Pearson publishes sample assessment material and past papers with mark schemes, which show how marks are given. In internal units, follow the criteria in your brief.

Is Harvard referencing required for BTEC National Engineering assignments?

Pearson's specification does not set a referencing system for the National. Your centre or Assignment Brief decides, so follow the style it names and cite your sources consistently.

Which Engineering units are mandatory at every size?

Unit 1 Engineering Principles and Unit 2 Delivery of Engineering Processes Safely as a Team are mandatory at every size. Unit 3 joins from the Extended Certificate, Unit 4 from the Foundation Diploma and Unit 5 from the Diploma. The Diploma and Extended Diploma versions add further mandatory units that depend on the pathway.
