If you believe your BTEC assessment was marked incorrectly, there is a formal route to raise it, but it isn't as simple as disagreeing with the grade you received. This page sets out what counts as valid grounds for an appeal, the step-by-step centre process, and what to realistically expect once you've submitted one.

What Counts as Valid Grounds for a BTEC Appeal?

Pearson's policy lets a learner appeal only where they believe the centre did not follow its procedures properly or applied them unfairly. The decisions that can be appealed are internal assessment results, decisions about reasonable adjustments and special consideration, and actions taken after a malpractice investigation. Simply disagreeing with the grade you received is not valid grounds for an appeal, and an appeal does not review your work unless it is concluded that the centre did not follow its procedures correctly or applied them unfairly.

The BTEC Centre Appeals Process, Step by Step

Start by raising the concern informally with your tutor or assessor first, since many issues resolve at this stage without a formal process. If that doesn't resolve it, submit a formal appeal under your centre's documented internal appeals procedure. A reviewer who wasn't involved in the original marking then examines the case, looking specifically at whether the process was followed correctly rather than re-marking from scratch.

Appeal Timing and Deadlines

Your centre sets the deadline for its own internal stage, so check the date with your exams office. Pearson's own stages have fixed limits: 14 calendar days from your centre's final decision to submit a preliminary appeal, and 14 calendar days from that outcome to request an appeal hearing. Late applications are not considered except in exceptional circumstances.

What Happens After You Submit an Appeal?

The outcome is either the original mark upheld or changed following review. An appeal is a fair re-examination of whether the process was followed correctly, not a guarantee of a grade increase, so it's worth going in with realistic expectations about what it can and can't achieve.

Taking an Appeal to Pearson After Your Centre: The Stages

Most appeals are resolved by the centre. Pearson will accept an appeal from a learner only after the learner has completed the centre's appeals procedure in full, and it asks for evidence that this has happened. Pearson's policy then sets out two stages:

  • Preliminary appeal (stage 1). Submit it within 14 calendar days of receiving your centre's final decision, using the appeals webform on Pearson's post-results services page. The application must clearly explain the grounds. Pearson writes with the outcome within 42 calendar days of receiving a valid application, and may reject applications that fall outside the permitted grounds.
  • Appeal hearing (stage 2). If you are not satisfied, request a hearing within 14 calendar days of the preliminary outcome. Pearson has up to 70 calendar days to arrange it. An independent panel reviews whether Pearson followed its own policies and processes correctly at the earlier stages. Hearings are usually conducted remotely, and you are invited to present your case. Pearson writes with the outcome within five working days.

The hearing is the final stage of Pearson's appeals process for vocational qualifications. There is no further route within Pearson.

What the policy covers, and what it does not

  • It applies to units that are internally assessed and externally verified. It does not apply to units that are internally assessed but externally moderated by Pearson, or to assessments that Pearson sets and marks, such as Pearson-set exams. Pearson's post-results services page covers those.
  • Appeals against a Pearson decision, such as an exam result, must be submitted through your centre, which makes the arrangements on your behalf.
  • Concerns outside the permitted grounds can go through Pearson's complaints process instead.
  • A fee is payable for appeals about assessment results. Fees for learners are paid in advance and refunded if the appeal is upheld.

Evidence your centre must keep

While a review or appeal is in progress, centres must keep all evidence related to the case. Pearson's current guidelines are to keep learner work for 12 weeks after certification and assessment documents for three years, and for longer if an appeal is still going on. If you plan to appeal, ask your centre in writing to keep your work and the assessment records.

Appeal vs Resubmission: What's the Difference?

An appeal challenges whether the original marking decision was correct. Resubmission is a second attempt at unmet criteria after a Referral, with no dispute about the original marking involved. Can I appeal a BTEC grade just because I disagree with it?

No. Valid grounds require showing a procedural irregularity or unreasonable exercise of academic judgement, not simple disagreement with the outcome.

Who reviews a BTEC internal appeal?

Typically someone at your centre who wasn't involved in the original marking, following your centre's documented internal appeals procedure.

How long does a BTEC appeal take?

This varies by centre, but appeals are generally handled within the centre's results timeline. Check your specific deadline and expected turnaround with your centre directly.

Can I still resubmit a Referred unit while an appeal is in progress?

In most cases yes, though the specific process depends on your centre. Clarify directly with your tutor or exams office so you don't miss a resubmission window while waiting for an appeal outcome.
