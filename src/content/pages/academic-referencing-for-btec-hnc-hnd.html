Harvard is the author-date referencing style that many BTEC Higher National centres ask students to use, but Pearson does not set it. This page explains what Pearson's Higher Nationals specification does say about referencing, how author-date citations and reference lists are normally laid out, and the mistakes that weaken an assignment. Your centre or Assignment Brief names the style you must use, so follow that first.

What Does Pearson Say About Referencing?

Pearson's Higher Nationals in Business specification (Issue 3, January 2022) does not name a referencing style. It says centres should give students a guide that explains how to use and reference source materials, including how to avoid plagiarism, and that all work submitted for assessment must be the student's own. It lists active research, effective writing, analytical skills and critical thinking among the study skills the Higher Nationals develop. The style itself, whether Harvard or another, is your centre's decision, so check your Assignment Brief or programme handbook.

BTEC Nationals are the same. Pearson's National specifications do not set a style either, and your centre may or may not ask for formal referencing. If your brief asks you to use sources, acknowledge them in the style it names.

In-Text Citations in the Author-Date (Harvard) Style

Author-date styles put the author's surname and the year in brackets next to the claim. The pattern is (Surname, Year) for a paraphrase, and (Surname, Year, p. number) for a direct quotation. For two authors, name both, for example (Surname and Surname, Year). For three or more, many centres use "et al." after the first author. Details differ between institutions, so check your centre's guide.

Here is a labelled placeholder rather than a real source: "Clear objectives help a team stay on task (Surname, 2021)."

The Reference List

The reference list at the end of the assignment gives the full details of every source cited, usually in alphabetical order by author surname. The pattern for a book is: Surname, Initial. (Year) Title in italics. Edition if not the first. Place of publication: Publisher. For a journal article it is: Surname, Initial. (Year) 'Article title', Journal Name, Volume(Issue), pp. first page to last page. For a website it is: Author or organisation (Year) Title. Available at: URL (Accessed: date).

A real example for a specification, following the book pattern: Pearson (2022) Pearson BTEC Levels 4 and 5 Higher Nationals in Business Specification, Issue 3. Pearson Education Limited.

Common Referencing Mistakes

These are our observations on what weakens assignments, not Pearson findings. Missing citations for paraphrased claims: students often cite direct quotes but forget that paraphrased ideas need a citation too. Incomplete reference list entries, such as a missing year or publisher. Entries in the list that do not match any in-text citation, or the reverse. Inconsistent formatting between the in-text citation and the list. Relying on general websites where your brief expects academic or industry sources.

These matter beyond formatting. A claim with no source can read as an unsupported assertion, which weakens an answer to a criterion that asks you to analyse or evaluate with evidence.

Struggling to apply this to your HNC or HND assignment? Referencing helps once you know which claims in your assignment need a source behind them. Check your brief for the style and the number of sources it expects.

Referencing and Academic Integrity

Pearson says the work submitted for assessment must be the student's own, and Pearson decides the sanctions for suspected malpractice that is reported to it. Plagiarism-detection software such as Turnitin is used by some centres, so correct citation is also an integrity safeguard. See BTEC academic malpractice policy.

Do I need to reference at BTEC National level?

Pearson's National specifications do not set a referencing style. Whether you need formal referencing depends on your centre and your Assignment Brief. If a brief asks you to use sources, acknowledge them in the style it names, and ask your tutor if it is unclear.

What referencing style does BTEC HNC and HND use?

Pearson does not set one. Harvard is a common choice at centres, but your centre or Assignment Brief decides, so follow the style it names. Pearson says centres should give students a guide on how to use and reference source materials and avoid plagiarism.

Do I need to reference every source, even websites?

Yes. Acknowledge any source you use to support a claim, including websites, in the style your centre names. Check your brief for the kinds of sources it expects, because general websites may be less suitable than academic or industry sources for an analytical or evaluative criterion.

Will I lose marks for incorrect referencing format?

It depends on your centre and the criteria in your brief. Small formatting slips matter less than missing citations: a claim with no source can leave a criterion that asks for evidence-based analysis unmet. Accuracy and consistency matter more than perfection of format, and your tutor can tell you how your centre treats errors.
